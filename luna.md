  <h1>The Power of Luna</h1>
  <p class="lead">A capable, modest model has become the tool I reach for most often when I am building.</p>
</div>

<div class="prose">
  <p>Luna is not the model with the biggest reputation or the most impressive-sounding specification. It is a practical workhorse, and I initially expected to use it for smaller tasks. In practice, I use it for about 90% of my development work.</p>

  <p>This website was built with Luna. My life-planner project was built entirely with Luna as well. Those projects have made the value of a model clear to me: the best tool is often the one that helps me keep moving, understand the problem, and make good decisions without adding unnecessary cost or ceremony.</p>

  <h2>Productivity without outsourcing the thinking</h2>

  <p>Many developers reach first for the largest model available. That can be the right choice for difficult or unfamiliar problems, but it is not automatically the right choice for everyday development. Luna gives me enough capability to work quickly while still requiring me to stay involved in the reasoning.</p>

  <p>That distinction matters. I am not trying to hand over the whole development process. I am trying to become better at planning, specifying, testing, and reviewing while using a tool that can turn those decisions into working code quickly. Luna supports that process without making every small change feel expensive.</p>

  <h2>Planning beats model size</h2>

  <p>The quality of a project depends heavily on the quality of its plan and specification. A large model cannot compensate for an unclear goal, a vague interface, or requirements that have not been thought through. Spending thousands of credits does not guarantee a better result, and in many cases it provides no obvious advantage in the finished work.</p>

  <p>Clear constraints, useful feedback, and deliberate iteration usually matter more. When I know what I am building and can describe the next step precisely, Luna is remarkably effective.</p>

  <h2>Using the right model for the job</h2>

  <p>This is not an argument for using Luna exclusively. I use other models for planning documentation and code review, where a different perspective or stronger reasoning can be valuable. Luna handles most of the implementation work: exploring an existing codebase, writing changes, fixing local issues, and iterating on the result.</p>

  <p>The useful lesson for me is not that one model is universally best. It is that a model does not need to be the largest or most expensive to become a serious development tool. Luna is fast, capable, and present in the work often enough that it has become part of my normal engineering workflow.</p>
</div>
